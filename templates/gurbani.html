📖 Sikh Heritage & History Explorer (ਇਤਿਹਾਸ ਤੇ ਫ਼ਲਸਫ਼ਾ)

1. Guru Nanak Dev Ji (1469 – 1539)

Founder of Sikhism. Composed Japji Sahib, Asa Di Var, and Sidh Gosht. Preached the oneness of the Creator and equality of humanity across 4 great journeys (Udasis). Established the 3 Golden Pillars: Naam Japna, Kirat Karo, and Vand Chhako.

2. Guru Angad Dev Ji (1504 – 1552)

Standardized and popularized the Gurmukhi script. Established youth physical fitness through Mall Akhara (wrestling grounds) and expanded Langar with Mata Khivi Ji.

3. Guru Amar Das Ji (1479 – 1574)

Composed Anand Sahib. Established Goindval Sahib with Baoli Sahib. Forbade Sati and Purdah, created the Manji system (22 administrative centers), and institutionalized 'Pehle Pangat, Phir Sangat'.

4. Guru Ram Das Ji (1534 – 1581)

Founded the holy city of Amritsar (Ramdaspur), excavated the sacred Sarovar, planned Sri Harmandir Sahib, and composed the sacred Lavan hymns for the Anand Karaj marriage ceremony.

5. Guru Arjan Dev Ji (1563 – 1606)

Compiled the Adi Granth in 1604, built Sri Harmandir Sahib with 4 doors open to all castes. Composed Sukhmani Sahib. First Sikh martyr (Shaheed), standing unwavering on burning plates for freedom of truth.

6. Guru Hargobind Ji (1595 – 1644)

Introduced the doctrine of Miri & Piri (temporal and spiritual sovereignty). Founded Sri Akal Takht Sahib in 1606. Transformed Sikhs into Saint-Soldiers and freed 52 kings from Gwalior Fort (Bandi Chhor Divas).

7. Guru Har Rai Ji (1630 – 1661)

Maintained a 2,200 horse cavalry without ever initiating war. Established a legendary botanical medicine dispensary and hospital at Kiratpur Sahib, exemplifying deep compassion for nature and living beings.

8. Guru Har Krishan Ji (1656 – 1664)

The 'Bal Guru' (Child Guru). Served victims of smallpox and cholera in Delhi at Gurdwara Bangla Sahib with divine selfless love, curing thousands.

9. Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji (1621 – 1675)

Revered as 'Hind Di Chadar'. Sacrificed his head at Chandni Chowk, Delhi to safeguard the religious freedom and conscience of Kashmiri Pandits, an unparalleled martyrdom for another faith.

10. Guru Gobind Singh Ji (1666 – 1708)

Created the Khalsa Panth on Vaisakhi 1699 at Anandpur Sahib through Khande Di Pahul. Authored Jaap Sahib, Chaupai Sahib, and Zafarnama. Bestowed eternal Guruship upon Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji at Nanded.

11. Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji (Eternal Living Guru)

1,430 Angs, 31 Classical Ragas. Universal Shabad Guru containing hymns of 6 Gurus, 15 Bhagats (Kabir, Ravidas, Farid, Namdev), 11 Bhatts, and 4 Gursikhs, guiding humanity toward universal love and truth.

Baba Ajit Singh Ji (18 yrs) & Baba Jujhar Singh Ji (14 yrs)

Battle of Chamkaur Sahib (December 1704): Eldest two sons of Guru Gobind Singh Ji. Led courageous sorties against overwhelming Mughal forces, defending the fortress of Chamkaur Sahib with superhuman valor and attaining glorious martyrdom on the battlefield.

Baba Zorawar Singh Ji (9 yrs) & Baba Fateh Singh Ji (7 yrs)

Fatehgarh Sahib, Sirhind (December 1704): The younger two sons. Kept in the freezing Cold Tower (Thanda Burj) with grandmother Mata Gujri Ji. Refused all royal bribes and threats from Nawab Wazir Khan to renounce their faith. Bricked alive with immortal courage for righteousness and human dignity.

1. Bhai Daya Singh Ji (Daya Ram from Lahore) — Compassion

First to offer his head at Vaisakhi 1699. Shopkeeper from Lahore, Punjab.

2. Bhai Dharam Singh Ji (Dharam Das from Hastinapur) — Righteousness

Second to step forward. Farmer from Hastinapur/Meerut, Uttar Pradesh.

3. Bhai Himmat Singh Ji (Himmat Rai from Jagannath Puri) — Courage

Third to step forward. Water-carrier from Jagannath Puri, Odisha.

4. Bhai Mohkam Singh Ji (Mohkam Chand from Dwarka) — Determination

Fourth to step forward. Calico printer/tailor from Dwarka, Gujarat.

5. Bhai Sahib Singh Ji (Sahib Chand from Bidar) — Sovereignty

Fifth to step forward. Barber from Bidar, Karnataka.

Sri Akal Takht Sahib (Amritsar, Punjab)

Founded by Guru Hargobind Ji in 1606. Supreme temporal seat of the Sikh Panth, facing Sri Harmandir Sahib.

Takht Sri Keshgarh Sahib (Anandpur Sahib, Punjab)

Birthplace of the Khalsa. Where Guru Gobind Singh Ji administered Amrit Sanchar on Vaisakhi 1699.

Takht Sri Damdama Sahib (Talwandi Sabo, Punjab)

Guru Ki Kashi. Where Guru Gobind Singh Ji dictated Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji to Bhai Mani Singh Ji.

Takht Sri Patna Sahib (Patna, Bihar)

Birthplace and childhood abode of Guru Gobind Singh Ji on the banks of the Ganges.

Takht Sri Hazur Sahib (Nanded, Maharashtra)

Sachkhand. Where Guru Gobind Singh Ji spent his final earthly days and consecrated Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji as eternal Guru in 1708.

ੴ Mool Mantar

ੴ ਸਤਿ ਨਾਮੁ ਕਰਤਾ ਪੁਰਖੁ ਨਿਰਭਉ ਨਿਰਵੈਰੁ ਅਕਾਲ ਮੂਰਤਿ ਅਜੂਨੀ ਸੈਭੰ ਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦਿ ॥

Ik Oankaar Sat Naam Kartaa Purakh Nirbhau Nirvair Akaal Moorat Ajoonee Saibhang Gur Parsaad.

One Supreme Reality, Truth by Name, Creative Being, Fearless, Without Enmity, Timeless Form, Unborn, Self-Existent, Realized by Guru's Grace.

The 5 Ks (ਪੰਜ ਕਕਾਰ)

• Kesh (ਕੇਸ): Uncut hair — accepting nature's divine perfection.
• Kangha (ਕੰਘਾ): Wooden comb — cleanliness and mental discipline.
• Kara (ਕੜਾ): Steel bangle — eternity, morality, and restraint.
• Kachera (ਕਛਹਿਰਾ): Modesty, fidelity, and preparedness.
• Kirpan (ਕਿਰਪਾਨ): Grace and defense of the vulnerable against tyranny.
